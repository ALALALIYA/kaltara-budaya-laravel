# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Indonesian high school (SMA) students aged 15–18, learning North Kalimantan arts and culture as part of the formal curriculum. They use the platform during class or as self-study — browsing materials, taking quizzes and exams, earning XP, and playing mini-games. Teachers and admins use separate panels to manage content and monitor progress.

Students want to feel like they're discovering something genuinely interesting, not grinding through a government worksheet. The job to be done: understand Kaltaran cultural heritage (Dayak, Banjar, Kutai, Tidung) well enough to pass exams — and ideally enjoy doing it.

## Product Purpose

Kaltara Budaya is a gamified interactive learning platform for North Kalimantan arts and culture. It replaces passive textbook reading with structured materials, adaptive quizzes, timed exams (daily / mid-term / final), mini cultural games, XP progression, and badges. Success means students finish a session knowing something they didn't know before — and want to come back.

## Brand Personality

Cultural depth + youthful energy. The platform takes the traditions seriously — Dayak, Banjar, Kutai, and Tidung cultures are honored, not reduced to clip art. But it speaks to a 16-year-old: energetic pacing, reward loops, game-like moments. Three words: **rooted, alive, rewarding**.

## Anti-references

- **Government edu portals**: stiff gray layouts, bureaucratic hierarchy, Times New Roman tables, official seals. This must feel like a product someone chose, not something issued to them.
- **Generic western edtech SaaS**: Inter font on white, purple-to-blue gradients, identical card grids with SVG icons, "modern minimalism" that has no cultural identity. The palette and feel must be unmistakably Kaltaran.
- **Childish primary-color apps**: Duolingo-bright cartoons or primary-color UI — students are in high school, not elementary school.

## Design Principles

1. **Culture is the texture, not the decoration.** Batik patterns, tribal motifs, and ethnic color references are structural — they're how the interface communicates where it's from. Not clip art sprinkled on top of a generic shell.
2. **Reward loops must feel earned.** XP bars, badges, and progress indicators are part of the learning contract. They should feel meaningful and precise, not inflated or childish.
3. **Legibility over atmosphere.** Dark forest-green backgrounds are identity-defining, but they must not sacrifice reading comfort. Contrast is non-negotiable on learning content: text, labels, and exam questions.
4. **Energy without noise.** Motion and color are present, but they serve focus — drawing attention to what matters, not competing with it. One animated element per screen surface is usually enough.
5. **Respect the four cultures equally.** Dayak, Banjar, Kutai, and Tidung each have their own color identity in the system (green, amber, red, blue). No culture is the "default."

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Body text must hit ≥4.5:1 against background. Exam and quiz content is high-stakes — poor contrast is a real barrier for students with color vision deficiencies. All interactive elements need visible focus states. Animations must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
